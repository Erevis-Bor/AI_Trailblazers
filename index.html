Across the Group, Trailblazers already exist. The opportunity is to identify them consistently, equip them to translate AI into everyday work, and create the conditions for that behaviour to spread.

Executive summary

Build a system around behaviour that is already emerging.

Trailblazers give us a distributed layer of AI enablement close to the work. The model needs to find the right people, create a common baseline, make their role usable in practice and learn from the effect they have around them.

01

Identify the right combination.

Look for experimentation, problem solving, peer credibility and responsible judgement rather than AI usage alone.

02

Equip beyond individual adoption.

Give Trailblazers the skills to use AI well themselves and diagnose opportunities in somebody else's work.

03

Mobilise them as a connected layer.

Give them manager-backed capacity, a peer community and clear routes into deeper expertise.

04

Scale through diffusion, then learn.

Grow coverage while tracking whether adoption, opportunity flow and value increase around active Trailblazers.

01 · Who are we looking for?

One profile. Five signals.

The existing definition gives us the direction. To identify Trailblazers consistently, we need to translate it into behaviours we can actually see.

The strongest candidates will not all look the same. The combination matters more than technical depth alone.

Signal 01

Curiosity & experimentation

They are already exploring. They test different approaches, learn through use and change course when something does not work. The sophistication of the example matters less than the instinct to try, reflect and improve.

Evidence might sound like

“I tried using Copilot for this task. The first approach was poor, so I changed how I structured it and now I use it every week.”

Why it matters

Trailblazers need enough practical experience to help colleagues through the messy first attempts, not just repeat training content.

The role

Apply. Translate. Share. Support. Surface.

Apply AI in their own work and keep learning through use.

Translate an abstract question into a clearer opportunity in somebody else's work.

Share prompts, workflows and examples so colleagues do not start from scratch.

Support local experimentation as an approachable first point of peer help.

Surface opportunities and barriers that need specialist, product or governance support.

02 · How do we find them?

Use several signals, then select for quality and coverage.

No single route can surface all of the qualities we care about. Nomination gives us context, self-identification catches people others may not see, and behavioural signals broaden discovery at scale.

Start with the people who can see the behaviour in context.

01

Start with nomination.

Some of the qualities we care about are inherently social. Leaders and peers can see who people already go to for help, who shares what they learn and who tends to spot better ways of doing things.

The prompts should describe behaviours rather than ask for a manager's “best people”. Peer nomination matters because informal influence is not always visible through the management line.

02

Keep the door open to self-identification.

Some strong candidates will not be nominated, and some managers may not yet know the experimentation happening in their teams. The route in should ask for evidence: what did you try, what problem were you solving, what did you learn, and what would you do next?

03

Use data to broaden discovery, not automate selection.

Tool engagement, learning participation, shared prompts or community activity can reveal pockets of AI-native behaviour that are otherwise hard to see. Usage volume is a signal, not a definition of a Trailblazer.

04

Assess the combination.

Heavy use without peer influence may indicate a power user. Technical depth without translation skills may indicate a builder. The Trailblazer role needs the combination.

05

Design the cohort for reach.

If Trailblazers cluster in a few digitally mature teams, the network may be excellent but still leave large parts of the Group unsupported. We should deliberately understand coverage across business areas, job families, seniority, location, working environment and AI maturity.

The aim is not a rigid quota. It is reasonable access to local support across the organisation.

03 · Equip

Create a common baseline, then build translation capability.

Trailblazers will arrive with different levels of AI literacy. The bootcamp gives them a consistent language and enough capability to use the tools well themselves. The additional step is teaching them to diagnose opportunities in somebody else's work.

01

Use the tools well

Available capabilities, strengths, limitations, prompting fundamentals and common failure modes.

02

Use them responsibly

Approved boundaries, data considerations, human review and when to stop and ask.

03

Diagnose the work

Outcome, friction, frequency, judgement and where the effort actually sits.

04

Know the next route

One-off task, reusable prompt, workflow, agent / developed solution, or specialist support.

Try the diagnostic

What is the simplest useful intervention?

This is the capability we want Trailblazers to build: understand the work first, then decide whether the answer is a prompt, a workflow, something more developed, or no AI intervention at all.

1. How often does the task occur?
2. What shape does the work take?
3. How much autonomy is required?
4. Are the responsible-use boundaries clear?

Start simple

One-off AI task

The task is occasional, the colleague remains in control and the work can be completed within one generative interaction. The Trailblazer can help structure the task and get a useful first result.

Trailblazer role

Coach the approach and help the colleague learn what good looks like.

04 · Mobilise

Make the Trailblazer a connection point, not a dead end.

The bootcamp starts the role; it does not sustain it. Trailblazers need three live connections around them and a foundation of manager sponsorship, time and recognition.

TrailblazerLocal translation layer
Connection 01

Support close to the work.

This is where most adoption support happens. Colleagues can bring an unfinished task, a poor prompt, a question about a tool or a vague “could AI help?” and work it through with someone who understands their environment.

What moves through this connection

Questions, demonstrations, reusable prompts and workflows, troubleshooting and confidence.

The foundation

Trailblazing needs to be treated as contribution, not extracurricular activity.

Manager sponsorshipManagers understand the role and why time is being allocated to it.

Protected capacityTime exists for experimentation, peer support, learning and community activity.

RecognitionUseful contribution is visible in development, performance and day-to-day acknowledgement.

05 · Scale

Scale the behaviour, not just the membership.

There are two things to grow: organisational coverage and the AI-native behaviours spreading around active Trailblazers. The second is ultimately the reason the first exists.

ColleagueSupported / adoptingTrailblazerEmerging Trailblazer

Start with a cohort distributed across different parts of the organisation.

01

Start distributed.

The first cohort should deliberately reach different business areas, role types, locations and levels of AI maturity. Coverage is part of the design, not something we inspect after selection.

02

Make useful behaviour visible locally.

Trailblazers apply AI in their own work, demonstrate practical examples and give colleagues a reusable starting point. Adoption becomes less abstract because people can see what good looks like in a familiar context.

03

Reduce the effort of trying.

Colleagues reuse prompts, adapt workflows and ask for help when the first attempt does not work. Repeat use matters more than a one-off trial because that is where new working habits start to form.

04

Watch for new Trailblazer behaviour.

Some supported colleagues begin experimenting independently, sharing what works and becoming people that others turn to. That gives us a natural pipeline into the next cohort.

05

Use each cohort to close gaps.

We can grow deliberately where coverage is weak, while the selection bar evolves as behaviours that feel advanced today become more normal across the Group.

06 · Measure

Measure the effect around them.

Network size, bootcamp completion and event attendance tell us whether the mechanism is running. The stronger test is whether adoption changes around active Trailblazers and whether that creates a better flow of useful opportunities and value.

Measure 01

Is the community healthy enough to work?

Coverage, active participation, ongoing learning, manager sponsorship and retention tell us whether the operating conditions are in place. They are enabling measures, not proof of adoption impact.

Value logic

Everyday AI and defined solutions need different evidence.

Released capacity

If AI reduces effort across many smaller activities, the immediate outcome is usually capacity rather than an automatic cash saving. We can evidence where that capacity is being released and whether it is sustained; the relevant business area then determines how it is used.

07 · Where we start

Prove the operating model before we maximise its size.

The first cohort is both an adoption intervention and a test. We should learn quickly enough to improve the next cohort rather than try to design the finished network upfront.
