AI Trailblazers

A fuller view of the operating model behind the AI Trailblazers memo.

The accompanying memo is the response to the brief. This appendix goes deeper into the design choices behind it: how we identify the right people, equip and support the community, turn local experimentation into wider adoption, and measure whether that activity creates value.

Scroll to read↓
00

Memo companion

The short answer, with the operating depth underneath.

The core idea is local translation: Trailblazers sit close enough to the work to help colleagues turn AI capability into practical change, while giving the organisation a better route to reuse what works and surface larger opportunities.

The model starts by identifying people who already combine experimentation, problem solving, peer credibility and responsible judgement. We find them primarily through nomination, test the evidence proportionately, and shape the cohort with organisational coverage in mind.

We then equip Trailblazers to move beyond strong personal use. A practical bootcamp gives them a common baseline and develops the capability to spot and shape opportunities in somebody else's work. Manager sponsorship, protected capacity, discoverability and specialist support make that role sustainable.

Wider adoption comes from three effects: colleagues get practical help at the point of need, useful approaches can be reused rather than rediscovered, and larger opportunities or blockers reach the teams that can act on them.

Measurement follows the same logic. We track whether the community is healthy, then whether adoption changes around it, whether useful practices and opportunities move through the network, and whether that activity contributes to business value.

01A

Identify · Profile

Start with the combination of behaviours we need.

A Trailblazer is not simply a heavy AI user or an advanced builder. The profile combines experimentation, problem and systems thinking, visible learning, peer credibility and responsible judgement.

Strong Trailblazer
Five signals. One overall judgement.

Signal 01

Curiosity & experimentation

What it looks like

Already exploring AI in real work. Tests approaches, learns through use, changes course when something does not work and wants to understand new capabilities rather than waiting for complete instructions.

Why it matters

The role depends on practical learning. We need people who are already comfortable experimenting and can talk about what they learned, not only what worked.

What we would listen for

A concrete example of something they tried, how the first attempt went, what they changed and what they would do next.

Three practical gates

Variation is useful. These three things are not optional.

01Existing experimentation

There is evidence that the person is already using approved AI in some form and learning through doing.

02Motivation to help others

They want to support adoption around them, not simply gain access to more training or tools.

03Responsible judgement

They recognise uncertainty, boundaries, human review and when to seek help.

02A

Mobilise & grow · The role

Make the contribution clear before we ask people to take it on.

Trailblazers need a practical role colleagues and managers can understand. The five parts overlap through real work, from experimenting themselves to helping others and surfacing opportunities that need to move.

01

Apply

Build practical credibility in their own work.

A Trailblazer continues using and experimenting with AI themselves. They test new capabilities, learn where the tools help and where they do not, and keep examples current.

Worked example

They develop a reliable way to use AI to prepare an initial weekly market update from a consistent set of source material.

What good looks like: their own work becomes raw material for demonstrations, reusable patterns and conversations with colleagues.

02

Translate

Help somebody else work out where AI actually fits.

A colleague asks whether the same approach could help with a recurring operational update. The Trailblazer does not simply hand over a prompt. They unpack the outcome, current process, inputs, repetition and judgement involved.

Worked example

The conversation reveals that part of the task is suitable for an AI-assisted workflow, while another part still needs human judgement and review.

What good looks like: the colleague leaves with a clearer problem, a practical next step and an appropriate level of ambition.

03

Share

Make useful learning reusable.

When something works, the Trailblazer captures enough context for somebody else to adapt it: the task, the approach, the prompt or workflow where relevant, what still needs judgement, and the limitations.

Worked example

The approach becomes a short worked example in the local AI hub rather than staying in one person's chat history.

What good looks like: the next colleague starts from evidence rather than a blank page.

04

Support

Coach people through the problem without becoming the doer.

Trailblazers act as an approachable first point of peer support when colleagues are experimenting, get stuck or are not sure how to approach something.

Worked example

A colleague brings an inconsistent prompt. The Trailblazer helps diagnose why it fails, demonstrates a better structure and explains the reasoning so the colleague can repeat it independently.

What good looks like: local support creates capability in the colleague rather than dependency on the Trailblazer.

05

Surface

Know when the opportunity needs to move.

Some questions are bigger than local support. Trailblazers shape the problem clearly enough to move it into the right specialist, product, build or governance route.

Worked example

Repeated questions expose a multi-step process used hundreds of times across a function. The Trailblazer helps describe the opportunity and routes it for triage rather than trying to build around system constraints themselves.

What good looks like: capability moves into teams while bigger opportunities and recurring barriers move back into the organisation.

Role boundaries

Useful local support without creating shadow IT.

Trailblazers are not informal IT support, governance approvers or delivery owners for every opportunity they identify. Their job is to help make useful progress, enable the colleague to become more capable, recognise the type of opportunity in front of them and know where the boundary of the role sits.

01B

Identify · Surface & assess

Find candidates, then test the evidence.

Nomination and self-identification surface the candidate pool. Assessment then tests whether the behaviours are really there, using a process that stays proportionate to cohort size.

Main discovery route

Nomination

Several of the qualities we care about are visible in context. Managers, leaders and peers can see who looks for better ways of doing things, who is already experimenting, who colleagues naturally ask for help and who shares what they learn.

Ask about behaviour, not status

Who is already experimenting with AI in real work?

Who spots friction that other people accept as normal?

Who do colleagues naturally approach when they are stuck?

Who shares what they learn, including when it did not work?

Peer nomination matters too: managers will not see every informal support interaction.

Second route

Self-identification

Some strong candidates will be experimenting in ways their manager has not seen or may simply be less visible. Self-identification gives them a route in without turning the process into an open-ended statement of enthusiasm.

Ask for evidence

What have you already tried?

What changed after the first attempt?

Where else could AI improve the way work is done?

Have you helped somebody else apply something similar?

A short evidence form should tell us how somebody thinks and what they are already doing.

Assessment

Keep the process consistent without making it bureaucratic.

Assessment should form a qualitative judgement about the overall shape of the person. We are not adding up a numeric score against five competencies.

For a smaller first cohort, a short structured conversation with each shortlisted candidate can add useful depth and help calibrate the model. If candidate volumes are materially higher, conversations should be targeted at borderline cases, conflicting evidence or a calibration sample rather than automatically applied to every candidate.

01Evidence form

One real example of AI use, what changed, what they learned, peer contribution and motivation.

02Profile judgement

Test the three gates and look for enough evidence across the wider profile to believe they can operate effectively.

03Evidence check

Use appropriate adoption, learning or community signals to corroborate the picture where useful data exists.

04Manager confirmation

Confirm the manager understands the role and can provide the agreed capacity.

05Cohort calibration

Review the cohort as a whole, resolve borderline cases and address obvious coverage gaps.

Profile judgement

A combination, not a score.

Practical gates

Existing experimentation

Can they describe real AI use, what happened and what they changed?

Responsible judgement

Do they recognise uncertainty, human review, boundaries and when to seek help?

Motivation to help others

Are they interested in supporting adoption around them, not only personal development?

Profile evidence

Problem & systems thinking

Do they start with the work and outcome before jumping to a preferred tool?

Peer credibility

Is there evidence that colleagues seek their help, learn from them or reuse what they share?

Visible learning

Can they share imperfect learning, adapt and explain what did not work?

Record the judgement qualitatively: evidenced / partly evidenced / not yet evidenced. The point is consistency and explainability, not a league table.
01C

Strong individuals still need organisational reach.

The final selection needs to work as a network. Coverage across business areas, roles, working environments and levels of AI maturity is part of selection, not an afterthought.

02B

Mobilise & grow · Bootcamp

Take Trailblazers one step beyond strong individual adoption.

The bootcamp creates a common baseline and develops the distinctive capability we need: being able to spot and shape AI opportunities in somebody else's work.

1Understand

Tools, concepts, boundaries and where support exists.

2Use

Apply AI effectively to individual tasks.

3Integrate

Use it independently and repeatedly across everyday work.

4Spot & shape

Diagnose opportunities in their own and other people's work.

Trailblazer baseline
5Build / transform

Create or help deliver developed solutions and redesigned workflows.

Trailblazer bootcamp

A common role, language and diagnostic approach.

The bootcamp should not be positioned as “advanced prompting”. It needs to make Trailblazers consistently useful in the role from day one.

01

Role & expectations

What a Trailblazer is expected to do, what sits outside the role, what good support looks like and how contribution is recognised.

OutcomeA shared role charter and clear boundaries.
02

Practical AI literacy

Approved tools, strengths and limitations, prompting principles, common failure modes and how capabilities differ.

OutcomeEnough baseline capability to use the tools confidently and explain them to someone else.
03

Responsible use

Data handling, appropriate use, human review, uncertainty, risk boundaries and routes for governance questions.

OutcomeConfidence to encourage experimentation without encouraging unsafe use.
04

Choosing the intervention

How to distinguish a one-off task, reusable prompt, workflow, automation, agent, developed solution or no AI at all.

OutcomeThe simplest sensible next step, without over-engineering.
05

Opportunity spotting

A structured diagnostic for understanding the outcome, current work, friction, judgement, repetition and where AI may help.

OutcomeA repeatable method for working through another person's task.
06

Peer enablement

How to coach rather than take over, demonstrate practically, adapt an approach and help colleagues become independent.

OutcomeSupport creates capability rather than dependency.
07

Support & escalation

How to resolve locally, reuse something proven, seek specialist advice or surface a larger opportunity.

OutcomeEvery Trailblazer knows what to do when the question moves beyond them.
08

Applied challenge

Each participant brings a genuine task or workflow and works it through using the diagnostic and support model.

OutcomeEvery Trailblazer leaves with a real example ready to use locally.

02C

Mobilise & grow · Discoverability

Make the network easy to find.

Local support only works when colleagues know it exists. Trailblazers need a visible route into the organisation rather than relying on personal networks.

Discoverability

Local support only works if people can find it.

A colleague should not need to already know a Trailblazer personally. The network should be searchable through the existing AI hub or support environment, reinforced through local manager and leadership communications and signposted from relevant AI learning and guidance.

02D

Mobilise & grow · Operating conditions

Create the conditions that make the role sustainable.

Manager sponsorship, protected capacity and recognition are not programme extras. They determine whether Trailblazers can contribute consistently rather than fitting the role around spare time.

01

Manager sponsorship

Manager sponsorship should be explicit rather than assumed. Managers need to understand why the colleague has been selected, what the role involves, what activity is expected and what support they are expected to provide.

A manager does not need to be an AI expert. They do need to create the conditions for the colleague to contribute, protect the agreed capacity and recognise the role as legitimate work.

In practice

Manager confirmation sits inside selection, then the relationship is checked again during the operating cycle rather than treated as a one-off permission.

02

Protected capacity

Trailblazer activity cannot depend entirely on spare time. Supporting colleagues, testing approaches, attending learning, sharing examples and bringing questions into specialist support all take time.

The exact allocation may vary by role and business area. We should not invent a universal percentage before understanding the operating context. What matters is that capacity is explicitly agreed, visible and reviewable.

Why it matters

Some of the strongest candidates may already be the people everybody asks for help. Without capacity, the programme formalises an existing burden rather than creating a sustainable role.

03

Recognition & reward

The contribution should be visible and valued without turning the network into a points competition. Useful recognition can come through objectives and development conversations, leadership acknowledgement, opportunities to showcase work, specialist development, pilots and small tangible recognition.

The message is simple: helping the organisation adopt AI is valued work, and the people doing it should feel that the organisation notices.

Avoid

Heavy gamification that rewards activity volume rather than useful adoption outcomes.

We ask Trailblazers to bringcuriosity · judgement · time · peer support↔In return, the organisation providescapability · capacity · specialist help · development · recognition
02E

Further detail

How the community stays healthy over time.

The bootcamp is a baseline. Ongoing learning, specialist support and periodic review keep the role useful as tools, policy and organisational priorities change.

Explore ongoing development, health checks and pathways
Targeted learning

Short sessions on new capabilities, agents, automation or recurring needs from the community.

Early exposure

Where appropriate, involve Trailblazers in pilots or previews before wider release.

Responsible-use updates

Refresh guidance when policy, controls or tool boundaries change.

Specialist office hours

Maintain a predictable route into deeper expertise and use recurring questions to shape future support.

Practical showcases

Use real work from the community to demonstrate what is changing and create reusable examples.

03

Accelerate wider adoption

Turn local support into measurable value.

Trailblazers help us move more quickly from AI awareness to useful application. They reduce the friction of trying AI in real work, make successful practice reusable, and give larger opportunities a clearer route into the teams that can act on them.

01

Local application

Help colleagues work through real tasks so useful AI becomes easier to try, repeat and improve.

More consistent use · less manual effort · better-quality outputs

Reuse

Capture prompts, workflows and lessons so the next team starts from evidence rather than from zero.

Less duplication · faster time to useful result

How questions move

Every question needs a sensible next route.

The network creates a two-way flow: capability and guidance move into teams, while opportunities, blockers and recurring questions move back into the wider organisation.

Route 01 · Resolve locally

The Trailblazer can help directly.

Use their own knowledge, a demonstration or a simple piece of guidance to help the colleague make progress. The aim is to leave the colleague more capable, not to become their permanent support desk.

What scale means

Scale coverage, reuse and independent capability.

Growth in the number of Trailblazers may support these outcomes, but membership is not the outcome itself.

01

Coverage

Use each cohort to close meaningful gaps so more colleagues can access relevant support close enough to the work to be useful.

Measure reach, not a neat one-per-X ratio.
02

Reuse

Move from “this worked for me” to patterns, examples, prompts and workflows that other people can adapt rather than rediscover.

The organisation becomes an incubator for useful practice.
03

Independent capability

People who initially need help should become increasingly able to spot opportunities, adapt approaches and help themselves.

Dependency should reduce as capability grows.

Value logic

Everyday AI and defined solutions should not be forced into the same ROI model.

Everyday AI

Released capacity across many small activities

Drafting, summarising, analysing, preparing, searching and communicating may each reduce effort without producing a discrete “project saving”.

MeasureReleased capacityTime / effort reductionSustained useEmployee confidenceChanged work patterns
Important: a reported 30 minutes saved is not automatically 30 minutes of salary saved. We can evidence released capacity without pretending it is already a realised cash benefit.

Defined solution

Harder attribution where the intervention is bounded

A specific workflow, agent, automation or process change can often be measured against a clearer baseline.

MeasureCycle timeThroughputManual effortQuality / errorsCost, risk, colleague or customer outcomes
Important: harder attribution is possible, but we still need a credible baseline and should separate AI contribution from wider process change.
05

First 90 days

Prove the model before optimising for scale.

The first 90 days should establish the model, select and prepare a deliberately distributed cohort, then activate it with enough baseline evidence to learn from the first cycle.

Days 1–30

Design the model

Agree the Trailblazer profile, role, manager expectations, selection approach and support routes.

Days 31–60

Select and prepare the first cohort

Run nominations and self-identification, select with coverage in mind, and confirm the capacity and specialist support available.

Days 61–90

Equip and activate

Deliver the bootcamp around real work, make Trailblazers visible, activate local support and establish the baseline measures for the first review.

Implementation details to confirm before launch

These do not change the recommendation, but they affect how the first cohort is implemented and should be resolved against the existing LBG operating environment rather than guessed in advance.

Initial cohort size & density

Enough coverage to test the model across different environments, but not so many people that the first cohort cannot be supported properly.

Expected time commitment

Protected capacity is essential, but the amount may vary by role and business area.

Behavioural data available

What usage and learning data exists, at what level it can appropriately be used, and what privacy or employee-relations considerations apply.

Existing specialist support routes

Connect Trailblazer escalation into existing AI, product, technology, risk and governance routes rather than creating parallel structures.

Recognition mechanisms

What can realistically be reflected in objectives, development, leadership recognition, pilots or tangible rewards.

Measurement baseline

Whether useful pre-activation baselines and sensible comparison populations can be established without overstating causality.

The test of the model

Capability has to spread beyond the Trailblazers.
